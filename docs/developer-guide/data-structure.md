# Data structure

## Implemented persistence

The repository persists Users, authentication infrastructure, Families, roleless Family Memberships, each User's nullable Current Family preference, Stores, reusable Store Sections, and their ordered associations. The initial migration creates `users`, `password_reset_tokens`, and `sessions`; separate migrations create `passkeys`, `families`, `family_memberships`, the `users.current_family_id` foreign key, `stores`, `store_sections`, the `store_store_section` association table, and the Store's `section_order_version`. Framework migrations also persist cache entries and locks, queued jobs, job batches, and failed jobs. See the [migration directory](../../database/migrations/), [`Family` model](../../app/FamilyAccess/Models/Family.php), [`Store` model](../../app/Cookbook/Models/Store.php), and [`StoreSection` model](../../app/Cookbook/Models/StoreSection.php).

Each Family has a name. Each Family Membership belongs to one Family and one User, is unique for that pair, and has no role column. Foreign keys cascade membership deletion when either side is deleted. Application behavior creates the Family and its first membership in one transaction. Removing or leaving the final membership is blocked, and account deletion is blocked while the User is the final member of any Family. Explicit Family deletion is the only operation allowed to remove its final membership.

`users.current_family_id` is a nullable foreign key that becomes null when its Family is deleted. Application code additionally verifies current membership and replaces a stale preference with another membership when one exists. Each Store has an explicit `family_id`, display `name`, application-generated byte-stable `normalized_name`, and optimistic `section_order_version`; `(family_id, normalized_name)` is unique, and deleting a Family cascades its Stores. A Store may also be hard-deleted only after it is resolved inside the authenticated User's `CurrentFamilyScope`. Each Store Section likewise has explicit Family ownership, display and normalized names with Family-scoped uniqueness, and a required seven-character `#RRGGBB` colour string. Deleting a Family cascades its Store Sections. `store_store_section` uniquely associates a Store and reusable Section and gives each association an unsigned position unique within its Store; deleting either record cascades the association. No Ingredient, Recipe, planning, nutrition, or Shopping List table or model exists yet, so no placement references exist to clear today. The connected Laravel Boost database is intentionally excluded as evidence by the approved documentation specification.

The conceptual model below applies the decisions to use [Family ownership](../adr/0003-scope-domain-data-to-families.md), [concrete purchasable Ingredients](../adr/0001-use-concrete-purchasable-ingredients.md), a [persistence-independent generator](../adr/0002-keep-shopping-list-generation-persistence-independent.md), and one [Laravel modular monolith](../adr/0004-build-a-laravel-modular-monolith.md). Capability workflows remain authoritative in [Family access](family-access.md), [Recipes and Ingredients](recipes-ingredients.md), [Stores and shopping order](stores-shopping-order.md), [Calendar planning](calendar-planning.md), and [Shopping List generation](shopping-generation.md).

## Proposed relational shape

> **Planned** — The following structure is conceptual. It names durable relationships and constraints approved in the final Domain Glossary chapter; it is not a migration contract. Concrete table and index syntax must be finalized and verified against both supported databases during implementation.
>
> - **Ingredients:** `ingredients` with canonical package quantity columns and
>   `ingredient_alternatives`. An Ingredient belongs to one Family, has either
>   positive `weight_grams` or positive `volume_millilitres` but not both, may
>   additionally have a positive `piece_count`, and must define at least one of
>   those quantities. It optionally references one Store and one Section valid
>   for that Store. The Store–Section pair is constrained through that
>   association. Each symmetric, non-transitive Alternative
>   relationship is stored once using an ordered, unique pair of Ingredient
>   identifiers from the same Family.
> - **Nutrition:** `ingredient_nutrition_profiles`. A profile belongs to an
>   Ingredient and stores an explicit basis quantity and unit plus kcal, fat,
>   protein, and carbohydrates.
> - **Recipes:** `recipes`, `recipe_ingredients`, `recipe_steps`, `recipe_tags`,
>   and `recipe_tag_assignments`. A Recipe belongs to one Family, has a positive
>   base Serving Count, and has one or more ordered Recipe Ingredients. Ingredient
>   lines may repeat. Steps are ordered. Tags are Family-scoped and many-to-many
>   with Recipes.
> - **Calendar:** `calendar_entries`. An entry belongs to one Family and
>   references a Recipe from that Family. Date, optional fixed Meal Label, and
>   positive Serving Count are stored. Recipe, date, and Meal Label form the
>   business uniqueness rule.
> - **Saved history:** `saved_shopping_lists` with relational ownership and
>   provenance headers plus an immutable versioned JSON payload. A saved list
>   belongs to one Family, is identified by generation timestamp, and freezes
>   output plus Calendar or Simple Plan provenance.
> - **Agent access:** a custom Sanctum personal-access-token model adds the
>   issuing User, fixed Family, display name, abilities, expiry, use, revocation,
>   and rotation metadata required by an Agent Credential. Plaintext secrets are
>   never persisted.
> - **Agent changes:** `agent_change_sets` carries indexed Family, credential,
>   issuer, lifecycle, request-identifier, digest, timing, outcome, and optional
>   supersession metadata. Versioned JSON stores the canonical request, preview
>   and effects, warning acknowledgements, identifier mappings, and final result.

## Ownership and authorization constraints

The implemented `family_memberships` table enforces uniqueness for `(family_id, user_id)`. Family creation and account deletion lock the affected User row first; account deletion then locks affected Families in identifier order, loads all membership counts in one query, and rejects deletion before persistence if any Family would be orphaned. Membership addition and removal lock all affected Users in identifier order before the Current Family. Family deletion locks all of its member Users in identifier order before the Family row. This shared order coordinates concurrent lifecycle changes. `CurrentFamilyScope` requires that validated Family before applying `whereBelongsTo` to a Family-owned model query; Store, Store Section, and both-record association resolution are its current Cookbook consumers.

> **Planned** — Continue persisting `family_id` on later Family-owned aggregate roots even when Family could be inferred through another relation. This makes request scoping, policy checks, and isolation queries explicit. Every cross-record reference must point to a record in the same Family; foreign keys alone may not express this rule, so application validation, scoped relationship queries, and targeted tests are required.
>
> Agent Credentials and Agent Change Sets also persist explicit `family_id`. Credential issuance verifies that issuer and Family have a live membership. Revocation, expiry, issuer deletion, or membership loss must make the credential unusable without consulting `users.current_family_id`. Change Set queries always constrain Family before credential or record identifiers.

## Names, archival, and deletion

Store and Store Section names use an application-generated byte-stable normalized key and database-backed Family-scoped uniqueness. Model mutators centralize display and key normalization for HTTP, factory, and action writes, while the creation and Store update actions convert unique-key races into field validation errors. Byte storage prevents MariaDB text collation from collapsing accent-distinct keys that SQLite keeps distinct.

> **Planned** — Reuse the normalized-key strategy for Ingredient, Recipe, and Recipe Tag names. The constraint includes archived Ingredients and Recipes.
>
> Recipes and Ingredients use reversible archival and restoration with no individual hard deletion in the MVP. Names, media, relationships, and existing references survive archival; only Family deletion ultimately removes these entities. Store and Store-Section removal intentionally nulls optional Ingredient placement as defined in the glossary. Database actions must match those semantics: deleting a Store clears both placement fields; removing a Section from one Store clears only the affected Section references in that Store; deleting the reusable Section entity removes all of its Store associations and clears its Section reference from every affected Ingredient while retaining each Store assignment. [ADR 0022](../adr/0022-archive-and-restore-recipes-and-ingredients.md) records the Cookbook lifecycle.

## Units and exact quantities

> **Planned** — Persist normalized Serving Counts, Recipe Ingredient quantities, package quantities, nutrition bases, and macro values as `DECIMAL(20,6)` rather than binary floating point. Reject normalized inputs with more than six fractional digits instead of silently rounding them. Convert those finite decimals into exact rational values for domain calculation, and quantize only at explicit presentation or export boundaries. Package Purchase Quantity remains the only whole-number result and uses the exact ceiling of the aggregated package requirement. [ADR 0013](../adr/0013-use-exact-rational-quantity-calculation.md) records this arithmetic boundary.
>
> Store package weight in grams or package volume in millilitres using mutually exclusive nullable columns, plus an independently nullable piece count. A database check requires positive values, forbids weight and volume together, and requires at least one populated quantity. Input adapters may accept another explicit metric unit but normalize it before validation and persistence; no display-unit preference column is stored. Presentation derives `g`/`kg` or `ml`/`l` using the 1000 threshold. Pieces have no selectable stored unit; `piece` is the canonical internal kind and the Czech interface renders `ks`, while optional wording is presentation text only. [ADR 0014](../adr/0014-use-one-universal-piece-unit.md), [ADR 0026](../adr/0026-store-one-canonical-metric-package-quantity.md), and [ADR 0030](../adr/0030-derive-metric-display-units.md) record these choices.
>
> Removing weight, volume, or piece count requires a dependency check against Recipe Ingredients and nutrition bases that use that canonical quantity kind, and cannot leave the Ingredient without any package quantity. A Recipe Ingredient persists its positive quantity in grams, millilitres, or piece count. Generator input therefore receives normalized values and converts the line directly into an exact package fraction without metric-unit parsing.

## Placement integrity

The implemented association owns a contiguous traversal position, so a shared Section can appear at different positions in different Stores. Complete-order rewrites lock one Store, validate the exact unique associated identifier set, and update all positions transactionally. Each Store has an optimistic order version, and a stale command is rejected without overwriting a concurrent member's change. [ADR 0023](../adr/0023-rewrite-contiguous-store-section-positions.md) and [ADR 0028](../adr/0028-reject-stale-store-section-reorders.md) record these mechanisms.

> **Planned** — An Ingredient has nullable Store and Store Section references. A database check requires Store whenever Section is present, and a composite foreign key from `(store_id, store_section_id)` to the unique Store–Section association guarantees that the pair is valid. Store-only placement leaves Section null. Application validation still resolves both records inside the Current Family, while transactional lifecycle actions clear references before an association, Store, or reusable Section is deleted. [ADR 0015](../adr/0015-enforce-store-placement-through-the-store-section-association.md) records the placement mechanism.

## Recipe and Calendar integrity

> **Planned** — Recipe Ingredient lines and Recipe Steps use contiguous positions. Ingredient lines deliberately do not have a uniqueness constraint on `(recipe_id, ingredient_id)`, because repeated Ingredients are allowed. Recipe Steps store plain text; Recipe Notes stay on the Recipe as a separate free-form field. Recipe edits submit the complete aggregate with an optimistic version, lock and validate it, and rewrite child state transactionally; stale submissions return fresh state for review. [ADR 0032](../adr/0032-save-recipes-as-versioned-aggregates.md) records this boundary.
>
> Recipe Tag assignments cascade when a Tag is hard-deleted after consequence confirmation. Recipe rows remain unchanged, and the normalized Family-scoped Tag name becomes reusable. [ADR 0033](../adr/0033-delete-recipe-tags-with-assignment-cleanup.md) records the lifecycle.
>
> A complete Recipe Nutrition Override is all-or-none across kcal, fat, protein, and carbohydrates and is expressed per serving. Enforce completeness in the domain and validation layers, and add a database check where supported.
>
> Calendar Entries store concrete dates and no recurrence fields. Persist one of the five Meal Label keys or the non-null internal `unlabeled` key, then enforce uniqueness for `(family, date, meal_label_key, recipe)`. Creating an existing combination atomically adds the submitted positive Serving Count to the existing row; editing onto an existing combination adds the edited entry's submitted Serving Count to the target and deletes the source. Every accepted request applies, including a transport retry, and the UI reports the resulting total. An entry whose Recipe is archived may change only Serving Count or be deleted. The persistence adapter maps `unlabeled` to the absent domain Meal Label, and a database check restricts the column to the six supported keys. Calendar Day and Simple Plan require no tables. [ADR 0019](../adr/0019-persist-an-unlabeled-calendar-key.md), [ADR 0024](../adr/0024-accumulate-duplicate-calendar-serving-counts.md), [ADR 0027](../adr/0027-restrict-edits-for-archived-recipe-calendar-entries.md), and [ADR 0029](../adr/0029-apply-every-calendar-accumulation-request.md) record these rules.
>
> Calendar Selection and Simple Plan are application inputs rather than extra persisted aggregates. Their adapters load and authorize the relevant Family-owned Recipes, then pass the same Recipe Selection contract to [Shopping List generation](shopping-generation.md#service-boundary).

## Immutable Shopping List history

> **Planned** — Saved Shopping Lists must survive later edits to Recipes, Ingredients, package definitions, names, Stores, Sections, alternatives, and Calendar Entries without recalculation. Persist the generated timestamp, source kind, provenance, grouping metadata, source-recipe breakdown, and every line's package count plus required, purchased, and surplus values in all units as snapshot values rather than live projections.
>
> Use relational columns for Family ownership, generation timestamp, source kind, and payload schema version, plus one immutable versioned JSON payload for the complete output and provenance. Encode exact rational quantities and internal canonical quantity kinds losslessly, and freeze localized labels and rendered values quantized to at most two fractional digits, so later calculation, localization, or presentation changes do not rewrite history. Every accepted save creates a separate row, even for identical content or a retried request, so storage needs a separate internal identity or timestamp tie-breaker while the timestamp remains the only user-facing identifier. Do not add normalized line children or duplicate the payload for relational analytics in the MVP. [ADR 0020](../adr/0020-store-versioned-shopping-list-snapshot-payloads.md) and [ADR 0025](../adr/0025-create-a-snapshot-for-every-save.md) record this boundary.
>
> The snapshot flow begins only after an explicit save of the transient generator result; [Shopping List generation](shopping-generation.md#transience-and-saved-snapshots) defines its output and provenance boundary.

## Agent Change Set persistence

> **Planned** — Persist only valid Agent Change Set previews. Invalid requests return structured validation errors without leaving history. A credential-scoped client request identifier and canonical-request digest enforce idempotency: the same identifier and payload resolve to the same record, while a different payload conflicts.
>
> Index the fields needed for Family authorization, credential ownership, lifecycle cleanup, and web-history filters. Keep operation details in versioned JSON rather than creating a generic mutation schema. Applied records are immutable provenance until a Family member deletes them; agents cannot delete them. Unapplied previews expire after 24 hours, and scheduled cleanup removes expired records. Credential revocation or rotation invalidates its unapplied previews.
>
> Catalog records expose ordinary `updated_at`, which mutations supply as their optimistic-concurrency expectation. This intentionally does not add a version column or aggregate fingerprint. Writes within database timestamp resolution, or nested writes that do not touch the aggregate root, may therefore escape detection; [ADR 0037](../adr/0037-use-updated-at-for-agent-concurrency.md) accepts that personal-project trade-off.
>
> Verify JSON encoding, identifier uniqueness, cleanup, transaction rollback, and timestamp comparison against both SQLite and MariaDB. [ADR 0010](../adr/0010-apply-previewed-agent-change-sets-atomically.md) and [ADR 0036](../adr/0036-use-a-deep-agent-integration-module.md) define the record lifecycle and module boundary.
